Nurse

A nursing resume is read for facts before it is read for prose: registration, setting, caseload, and whether you have worked the kind of ward doing the hiring.

7 min readUpdated 11 September 2026Written by the PlainSheet team

What gets looked at first

Before anyone reads a sentence, they check whether you clear the bar. For this role that means:

  • Your registration number and its expiry, near the top. Missing it stalls the whole application.
  • The setting: acute, community, theatre, ICU, care home. They are different jobs.
  • Patient load and ratio, because it says more about your week than any adjective.
  • Current mandatory training and specialist certificates, with dates.
  • Whether you have precepted or supervised anyone.

Everything on the page below is there to answer one of those, or it is not there.

The example

Invented, but built the way a good one is. Read it once for the shape, then read the notes underneath.

Registered nurse, seven years, acute medical and respiratory. Currently band 6 on a 28-bed respiratory ward, precepting two newly qualified nurses. Looking for a band 6 post in respiratory or acute medicine.

Why those lines and not the usual ones

Every weak version below is a real sentence that appears on thousands of resumes. Nothing was invented to fix them — the same facts are simply stated.

Says little

Provided high-quality care to patients on a busy ward.

Says the same thing, usefully

28-bed respiratory ward, typical allocation of six to eight patients, including NIV and tracheostomy care.

Says little

Helped to improve the discharge process.

Says the same thing, usefully

Rewrote the ward discharge checklist with the pharmacy team after repeated delays over TTOs; average discharge time moved from mid-afternoon to before midday.

The pattern is the one from the bullet points guide: what you did, how you did it, and what changed as a result.

Common questions

Where do I put my registration number?

In the contact block at the top, with its expiry date. Recruitment cannot progress an application without checking it, and hunting for it delays you.

Should I list every mandatory training module?

Only the current ones, grouped into one line. The full matrix belongs in the interview folder, not on the page.
