Sales representative

Sales is the one field where the resume is expected to contain numbers, and the one where candidates most often leave them out.

6 min readUpdated 11 September 2026Written by the PlainSheet team

What gets looked at first

Before anyone reads a sentence, they check whether you clear the bar. For this role that means:

  • Quota and attainment, per year, as a percentage.
  • Average deal size and sales cycle length — they define the job more than the title.
  • New business or account management, because they are different skills.
  • Who you sold to: job title and company size of the buyer.
  • Whether you have carried a team quota as well as your own.

Everything on the page below is there to answer one of those, or it is not there.

Why those lines and not the usual ones

Every weak version below is a real sentence that appears on thousands of resumes. Nothing was invented to fix them — the same facts are simply stated.

Says little

Consistently exceeded sales targets and built strong client relationships.

Says the same thing, usefully

Quota €1.4m new business: 118% in 2025, 104% in 2024, 96% in 2023. Average deal €62k, cycle around four months.

Says little

Responsible for prospecting and developing a sales territory.

Says the same thing, usefully

Built the territory from three named accounts to a pipeline of forty; two of the first five became the company's largest customers.

The pattern is the one from the bullet points guide: what you did, how you did it, and what changed as a result.

Common questions

What if I missed quota one year?

Include it. A run of 118%, 104%, 96% reads as an honest record; three identical "exceeded target" claims read as a template. Be ready to say what happened.

Can I name my customers?

Name the type — "manufacturers of 200 to 2,000 staff" — unless the logo is public and your employer would not mind. Naming accounts from a confidential pipeline is a poor signal to your next employer.
