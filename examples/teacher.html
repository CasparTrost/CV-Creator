Teacher

A teaching application is read by someone who will have to cover your timetable. Subjects, key stages and what you can teach beyond your specialism come first.

7 min readUpdated 11 September 2026Written by the PlainSheet team

What gets looked at first

Before anyone reads a sentence, they check whether you clear the bar. For this role that means:

  • Subjects and key stages or year groups, immediately. This is timetabling, and it decides whether they read on.
  • Your qualified status and the year you got it.
  • Whether you can offer a second subject, and to what level.
  • Responsibilities beyond the classroom: form tutor, subject lead, clubs, trips.
  • Evidence about outcomes, phrased as what you changed rather than what the cohort was.

Everything on the page below is there to answer one of those, or it is not there.

Why those lines and not the usual ones

Every weak version below is a real sentence that appears on thousands of resumes. Nothing was invented to fix them — the same facts are simply stated.

Says little

Passionate and dedicated teacher committed to raising standards for all pupils.

Says the same thing, usefully

Rewrote the KS4 scheme of work around retrieval practice and a common assessment calendar. GCSE grade 5+ in combined science moved from 48% to 61% over three years against a broadly stable intake.

Says little

Responsible for mentoring less experienced colleagues.

Says the same thing, usefully

Line-manage four teachers including two ECTs, with weekly coaching meetings and paired lesson observations.

The pattern is the one from the bullet points guide: what you did, how you did it, and what changed as a result.

Common questions

Should I include exam results?

Include the change you were responsible for, with enough context to be honest about the intake. A raw percentage with no baseline tells a head of department nothing and they know it.

How much detail about behaviour management?

One concrete example is worth a paragraph of claims. What you did with a specific difficult group, and what changed, belongs in the cover letter more than the resume.
