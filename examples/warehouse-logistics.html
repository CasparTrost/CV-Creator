Warehouse and logistics

Warehouse hiring is quick and practical. Licences, systems and numbers decide it; a paragraph about being a hard worker does not.

6 min readUpdated 11 September 2026Written by the PlainSheet team

What gets looked at first

Before anyone reads a sentence, they check whether you clear the bar. For this role that means:

  • Licences and their expiry: counterbalance, reach, PPT, VNA, LGV class.
  • The WMS you have used. SAP EWM, Manhattan, Körber, in-house — say which.
  • Throughput: picks per hour, lines per day, pallets, dispatch window.
  • Shift pattern experience, including nights.
  • Whether you have run a team, and how many.

Everything on the page below is there to answer one of those, or it is not there.

Why those lines and not the usual ones

Every weak version below is a real sentence that appears on thousands of resumes. Nothing was invented to fix them — the same facts are simply stated.

Says little

Worked as part of a busy warehouse team picking and packing orders.

Says the same thing, usefully

Run a shift of fourteen pickers and two forklift drivers, 4,000 to 6,000 lines a night, dispatch cut-off at 05:00.

Says little

Helped to reduce errors and improve accuracy.

Cut mis-picks from around 4% to under 1.5% in two months by replacing the paper handover with a five-minute standing briefing and a one-page sheet.

Common questions

Do licence expiry dates need to be on the resume?

Yes. An expired counterbalance licence is the difference between starting Monday and starting in six weeks, and the shift manager would rather know now.

I have worked through several agencies. How do I show that?

Group them: "Agency roles through Randstad and Adecco, 2019 – 2021: three warehouse contracts, longest fourteen months at …". A list of six short entries looks unstable; one grouped line is honest and reads calmly.
